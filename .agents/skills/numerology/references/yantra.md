# Yantra Reference — What It Is and How to Use One

> **Cultural / devotional tradition, not prediction.** A yantra is a traditional
> meditation and worship aid. Nothing here is medical, financial, or legal advice,
> and a yantra is not a substitute for real-world action or professional help.
> Treat it as a focusing tool and a cultural practice.

## What is a yantra?

A **yantra** (Sanskrit *yantra*, "instrument / device") is a geometric diagram
used in the Hindu and Tantric traditions as a focal point for meditation, prayer,
and intention. Where a **mantra** is sound and a **murti** is an image, a yantra is
*sacred geometry* — a map of an energy, deity, or planet drawn as lines, circles,
petals, and numbers.

This skill generates **numeric yantras** — yantras whose heart is a **magic
square**: a grid in which every row, every column, and both diagonals add up to the
**same total** (the "magic sum"). The unbroken balance of that grid is the point:
no matter which way you read it, the energy "adds up." Three kinds are produced:

| Yantra | Grid | Built from | Use |
|--------|------|-----------|-----|
| **Janma / birth yantra** | 4×4 | your date of birth (top row = day, month, century-part, year-part) | a personal focusing diagram tied to *you* |
| **Navagraha (planetary) yantras** | 3×3 | the classical Lo Shu square, one per graha | to strengthen a specific planet's good qualities |
| **Custom-number yantra** | 3×3 | any total you choose (a "lucky number" × 3, etc.) | a focus object for a chosen intention |

### Why a magic square?

A 3×3 magic square's total is always **3 × its centre**, so the centre number is
the "seed" and the whole grid radiates balance around it. The 4×4 birth square is
richer: its top row literally spells your birth date, and the rest of the grid is
arranged so the date's total repeats in every direction — a tidy symbol of a life
"in balance" around the moment you were born. (For very small birth months a couple
of cells can be zero or negative; that is mathematically fine — the square is still
perfectly magic.)

## The nine planetary (Navagraha) yantras

Each graha's yantra is the Lo Shu square shifted so its rows/columns/diagonals hold
a fixed total. **Surya (Sun) = the classical Lo Shu square, magic sum 15**; each
later graha lifts the centre by one. Pick the planet whose qualities you want to
strengthen — often the one that is weak, afflicted, or running in your current
dasha/transit (the vedic-astrology skill can tell you which).

| Graha | Planet | Day | Magic sum | Helps with |
|-------|--------|-----|-----------|------------|
| Surya | Sun | Sunday | 15 | vitality, confidence, authority, health, father, government |
| Chandra | Moon | Monday | 18 | peace of mind, emotions, mother, sleep, public goodwill |
| Mangal | Mars | Tuesday | 21 | courage, energy, land/property, drive, disputes |
| Budh | Mercury | Wednesday | 24 | intellect, speech, study, trade, communication |
| Guru | Jupiter | Thursday | 27 | wisdom, wealth, children, teachers, dharma |
| Shukra | Venus | Friday | 30 | love, marriage, comforts, art, vehicles |
| Shani | Saturn | Saturday | 33 | discipline, longevity, relief from delays, justice |
| Rahu | — | Saturday | 36 | sudden gains, foreign matters, calming anxiety |
| Ketu | — | Tuesday | 39 | detachment, spirituality, relief from hidden troubles |

> **Honesty note on tradition.** Several different planetary magic squares circulate
> in different books. This skill uses one consistent, reproducible construction (the
> constant-offset method from the Lo Shu base). What every tradition agrees on is the
> *principle* — a balanced square held with a focused mind and the planet's mantra —
> not one single "official" grid.

## How to use a yantra — a simple, honest practice

You do **not** need to be initiated, buy anything, or believe anything in particular
to use a yantra as a meditation aid. Here is a clean, traditional-but-accessible way.

### 1. Get the yantra in front of you
- Print the SVG (or keep it on a screen). Many people frame it or place it on a small
  altar, a clean shelf, or a desk. Paper, copper, or brass are traditional materials.
- **Direction:** sit facing **east** or **north**; place the yantra so you look at it
  level with or slightly below your eyes.

### 2. Choose your timing
- A daily few minutes is worth far more than one big session. **Morning, after a
  bath, on an empty stomach** is the classic slot.
- For a **planetary** yantra, its **own weekday** (see the table) and the hour around
  sunrise are considered most favourable. Start on that day if you can.

### 3. Settle and set intention (sankalpa)
- Sit comfortably, spine easy, take a few slow breaths.
- Silently state a simple, honest intention — e.g. *"May my mind be steady and
  clear"* for Budh, *"May my home have peace"* for Chandra. Keep it positive and
  realistic; the yantra focuses effort, it does not replace it.

### 4. Trataka — soft gazing
- Rest your eyes on the **centre** of the square and let your gaze soften. Take in the
  whole diagram without straining. When the mind wanders, return to the centre.
- 3–11 minutes is plenty. If your eyes tire, close them and hold the after-image.

### 5. Japa — repeat the mantra
- Repeat the **bija (seed) mantra** for the planet (printed on each planetary yantra),
  or simply *"Om"*, while gazing or with eyes closed.
- A traditional count is **108 repetitions** (use a mala/rosary if you have one). Any
  steady count you can keep is fine — consistency beats quantity.

### 6. Close with gratitude
- Sit quietly for a few breaths, offer a simple thanks, and carry the calm into your
  day. Some people offer a flower, incense, or a lamp; none of this is required.

### Optional: "energising" (prana pratishtha)
In temple practice a yantra is formally consecrated by a priest. For personal use,
your own **regular, sincere attention is the energising** — a yantra you actually sit
with daily becomes meaningful; one in a drawer does not.

## Do's and don'ts (practical, non-superstitious)

- **Do** keep it clean and treated with respect — it is a focus for your own
  discipline as much as anything.
- **Do** pair it with real action. A Budh (Mercury) yantra plus actually studying
  beats either alone.
- **Don't** expect it to *replace* medical, financial, or legal help. If something
  is genuinely wrong, get qualified help — and use the practice for steadiness.
- **Don't** worry about "doing it perfectly." Sincerity and regularity are the whole
  technique.
- **Don't** spend large sums on "guaranteed" yantras. A printed square you use daily
  is worth more than an expensive one you ignore.

## Which yantra should I pick?

- **Want a personal object tied to you?** → the **birth (Janma) yantra** from your DOB.
- **Want to strengthen a specific area** (study, courage, peace, wealth, marriage,
  discipline)? → the matching **planetary yantra** from the table above.
- **Working with a weak/afflicted planet or a running dasha?** → ask the
  **vedic-astrology** skill which graha needs support, then use that planet's yantra.
- **Want a focus for a particular number/intention?** → a **custom-target** 3×3 square.

> Reminder: this is a contemplative and cultural practice. It is **not predictive**
> and should never be used to make consequential decisions on its own.
